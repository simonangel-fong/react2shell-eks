**From 58% to 93%: What Failing the CKS Exam Taught Me About Engineering 🚀**

I failed my first Certified Kubernetes Security Specialist (CKS) exam with a score of 58%.

On my second attempt, I passed with **93%**. 🎉

What changed? The biggest change was how I practiced and allocated my time under pressure.

Here are 3 lessons I took away:

**1️⃣ Practice beats memorization.**

The CKS exam is hands-on and time-limited. Official docs are available, but knowing where to look—and why a configuration works—comes from practice, not just reading.

**2️⃣ The environment matters.**

On my first attempt, I spent 12–15 minutes on a task that normally took 5–6. I was fighting the remote desktop UI and copy/paste shortcuts instead of solving the problem. Before my retake, I practiced repeatedly in KodeKloud labs.

**3️⃣ Trade-offs beat perfectionism.**

I lost valuable time fine-tuning audit rules while leaving easier questions unfinished. On my retake, I prioritized achievable tasks, broke complex ones into steps, and moved on when necessary.

That last lesson goes beyond certification: **DevOps and platform engineering are about making good decisions under real constraints.**

But a certificate is only the beginning.
