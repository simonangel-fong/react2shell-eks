**From 58% to 93%: What Failing the CKS Exam Taught Me About Engineering 🚀**

I failed my first Certified Kubernetes Security Specialist (CKS) exam with 58%.

On my second attempt, I passed with **93%**. 🎉

Same exam. Same two-hour time limit. A completely different approach.

Here are three lessons I learned along the way.

**1️⃣ Practice beats memorization.**

CKS is a hands-on exam. Having access to the official Kubernetes documentation doesn't mean you can solve every task efficiently.

It's not just about knowing which commands to run. It's about understanding **why, when, and how** to apply them.

For example, upgrading a Kubernetes cluster requires understanding version compatibility, control plane and worker node responsibilities, and the differences in their upgrade procedures.

Documentation is a reference, not a substitute for hands-on experience.

**2️⃣ Don't underestimate the exam environment.**

During my first attempt, I spent 12–15 minutes on a task that normally took me 5–6 minutes.

Why? I struggled with the remote desktop UI, especially copy-and-paste shortcuts between the terminal and Firefox.

A small technical inconvenience became a serious time-management problem.

For my retake, I repeatedly practiced with KodeKloud labs to build familiarity with both the tasks and the environment.

**3️⃣ Engineering is about making trade-offs, not chasing perfection.**

This was my biggest lesson.

During my first attempt, I spent too much time perfecting Kubernetes audit logging rules while leaving easier tasks unfinished.

In a two-hour exam, every minute matters.

For my retake, I changed my strategy:

- Prioritize tasks I can complete confidently.
- Break complex tasks into smaller, achievable steps.
- Know when to move on and return later.

**Trade-offs aren't about giving up. They're about making better decisions under constraints.**

And that's a lesson that applies far beyond an exam—to DevOps, Platform Engineering, and production incident response.
